This is Pilot 1 of a new visual style — a deliberate calibration exercise,
NOT a finished marketing video. See ~/Workspaces/skills/manim-video-quality/reference/style-3b1b-google.visual-style.md and
~/Workspaces/skills/manim-video-quality/reference/storytelling-vocabulary.md for the full spec.

Intent: adopt 3Blue1Brown's dark, patient, math-explainer visual grammar
(near-black background, thin unfilled/low-opacity shapes, generous negative
space, slow deliberate motion, minimal text) for Google Agent Platform
technical content, with Google's exact brand colors reserved ONLY for
semantic confirm/blocked states (here: Google Green appears exactly once,
for the moment identity is issued) rather than general decoration.

This is intentionally sparse and slow — that is the design goal, not a
defect. Evaluate whether it successfully achieves calm, patient,
idea-first pacing (the 3b1b feel), not whether it has high information
density or a marketing hook (it deliberately has neither yet — this is a
10-second isolated beat being used to calibrate technique, not a full video
with a call to action).

Do not penalize: minimal on-screen text, long stretches with no text at
all, slow pacing, mostly-empty frame, absence of a call-to-action, absence
of a title card, or short overall length. These are the explicit goals of
this pilot.

DO evaluate: does the color discipline read clearly (blue = generic agent,
gold = special/issuer entity, green = the one reserved "confirmed" moment)?
Does the camera push-in feel purposeful? Does the pacing feel patient/calm
rather than merely slow-for-no-reason? Is the single caption at the end
legible and well-timed?
